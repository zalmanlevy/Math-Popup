Overview

Math Popup is a small always-available notepad. By default every line is plain text, so it works as a tray-resident scratchpad out of the box. Mark any individual line as math and it's evaluated live, with the result shown right on the line — so a single note can freely mix prose, lists, and calculations.

The window lives in the system tray. Click the tray icon to toggle it, or press Ctrl+Alt+M from anywhere.

Window controls

The header from left to right:

  • 📄 Tabs — open the tab bar to switch between notes and add, rename, reorder, or close them. See Tabs below.
  • 🕒 Recently Closed — reopen a tab you closed by mistake. Hover to see the list of recently closed notes.
  • Drag handle — click and drag anywhere in the empty part of the header to move the window.
  • 📌 Pin — keep the window above other apps. Highlights when active.
  • ƒ Variables — hover to see every variable currently defined in the note and its current value. Details in the Variables popup section below.
  • ? — opens this help page.
  • ⚙ Settings — opens Settings. A small blue dot appears here when an update has been downloaded and is ready to install.
  • × — hides the window. The app stays in the tray.

The window is resizable from any edge. Position and size are remembered between sessions.

Tabs

Keep several independent notes open at once, each with its own content. Click 📄 in the header to toggle the tab bar that slides in just below the toolbar; whether it's expanded is remembered between launches.

  • Switch — click a tab, or cycle with Ctrl+Tab / Ctrl+Shift+Tab. The active tab is highlighted.
  • New tab — click the + on the right of the bar, or press Ctrl+T.
  • Close — click the × on a tab, or press Ctrl+W for the current one.
  • Rename — right-click a tab and choose Rename, or press Ctrl+L.
  • Reorder — drag a tab along the bar, or use Reorder from the right-click menu (also on the overflow chevron).
  • Overflow — when there are more tabs than fit, a chevron showing the number of hidden tabs appears next to +; hover or click it for the rest. If the tab you're on lives in the overflow, it's surfaced into the bar with a small marker before it — hover the marker to confirm there are more tabs hidden in between.

Restore a closed tab. Press Ctrl+Shift+T to reopen the most recently closed tab — press it again to keep reopening older ones. You can also press Ctrl+Z immediately after closing a tab to bring it back, or open the 🕒 Recently Closed list in the header and pick one.

Math & text lines

Every line is text by default. Mark an individual line as math and it's evaluated, with its result shown inline. A note can mix the two freely — prose and lists stay text while the lines you care about compute.

Make a line math (or text again) in either of these ways:

  • Double-click its line number in the left gutter to toggle that line between math and text.
  • Type /math or /text on the line and press Space or Enter — the command word is removed and the line switches. (They're also in the slash menu.)

The line number shows the type: math lines get a green, underlined number; the line your cursor is on is filled in (green for math, grey for text).

Wrapped lines are marked. When a line is long enough to wrap onto more than one row, a faint marker beside its number grows from a small dot into a short bar spanning the wrapped rows — so you can tell at a glance that those rows are one continued line.

Enter carries the type forward. Pressing Enter makes the new line match the line you came from, so a run of math lines stays math until you switch one back to text (and vice-versa).

The result is inline. A math line's answer appears as a green = … pinned to the right, and the = signs line up in a column. Hover it (or put your cursor on that line) to highlight it, and click to copy the value. A long formula wraps before the result instead of running underneath it, and any error shows right after the =.

Numbers & formatting

  • Type any expression: (12 + 5) * 3 → 51.
  • Bare decimals get a leading zero automatically: .5 → 0.5.
  • Numbers with 4+ digits get commas inserted on space, operator, Enter, or Tab: 1000 → 1,000. Toggleable in Settings.
  • Commas in a number are purely cosmetic — their position doesn't matter, so 1,000, 10,00, and a half-typed 10,000, all evaluate the same. (Commas that separate function arguments, like min(1, 2), are still respected.)
  • Pressing Space or Tab with the cursor in a number re-tidies its commas and hops to a single space after it — reusing the space that's already there instead of adding a second.
  • Results are formatted with commas and trimmed to your decimal cap.

Currency symbols

Money symbols are recognized inside expressions and ignored when the line is evaluated, so you can write what feels natural and still get a result:

  • $50 + $20 → 70
  • £1,000 * 1.05 → 1,050
  • €500 - 5% → 475

Recognized: $ € £ ¥ ₹ ₽ ¢ ₩ ₪ ₫ ₴ ₸ ₺ ฿. They render with a muted color in the editor so you can see they're decorative.

Quoted text (notes)

Anything wrapped in single '…' or double "…" quotes is treated as a note and completely ignored by the math, so you can annotate a line and still get a clean result:

  • 5 * 2 '234' → 10 (the quoted part is dropped)
  • 1000 + 250 "deposit" → 1,250
  • 'Invoice 1291 — confirm it's 1.9' → no result; the whole line is just a note (it shows blank — no result — rather than echoing the text).

Two conveniences make this painless:

  • Numbers inside quotes are left exactly as typed — they don't get auto-commas, since they're text, not math.
  • Only the outermost pair of quotes is matched, so an apostrophe in the middle (like it's) won't break the line.

Directives

Directives are special lines starting with / that change how the lines below them are rendered. They produce no result themselves and are styled with a colored pill so they're easy to spot.

  • /no_dec_limit — overrides your Max decimal places setting for everything below this line. Results show their natural number of decimals (trailing zeros are stripped) capped at 6, BA II Plus-style. Use it when you need more precision for one section without opening Settings.
  • /clear — wipes the entire note (useful when you've finished a calculation and want a blank slate).

Example with Max decimal places set to 2:

10 / 3        → 3.33
/no_dec_limit
10 / 3        → 3.333333
1 / 2         → 0.5
6             → 6

The /no_dec_limit directive must be on its own line and applies until the end of the note — there's no reverse directive yet.

Slash command menu

Type / anywhere a word can start — at the beginning of a line or right after a space — and a popup appears with the available commands. (A / used as division, like 5/2, doesn't trigger it.) Continue typing to filter, use ↑/↓ to move, and Enter or click to confirm. Esc dismisses it.

The menu also flips above the caret when there isn't enough room below, and shows up to 5 items at a time (longer lists scroll).

Available commands: /math and /text (switch the line's type — see Math & text lines), /no_dec_limit, and /clear — the latter two are covered under Directives above.

Suffixes (k, m, b, t)

Type a number followed by a suffix, then press space — the editor expands it to the full number:

  • 5k → 5,000
  • 1.5m → 1,500,000
  • 2b → 2,000,000,000
  • 3t → 3,000,000,000,000

You can add or edit your own suffixes in Settings → Custom suffixes. Each suffix has a symbol, a multiplier, and a case-sensitivity flag.

If you turn off "Expand suffixes in editor", the typed text stays as 5k but the evaluator still computes the right value.

Smart Tab keeps the suffix attached. When you press Tab with the caret inside a number that has a trailing suffix (e.g. 10000k), the caret jumps past the entire token — the k is treated as part of the number, not separated by a space.

Variables

Define a variable on its own line, then reference it later:

price = 12.50
qty   = 7
price * qty

Variable names use letters, digits, and underscores, and must start with a letter or underscore. Reserved names like L1, pi, e, true, and false cannot be redefined — trying to assign to one shows Reserved after the = with a hover tooltip.

Variables popup (ƒ)

The ƒ button in the title bar opens a hover popup listing every variable currently defined in the note, along with its latest value and the line it was assigned on. Hover the button to open it; move the cursor onto the popup to keep it open.

Each row shows:

  • The variable name (with a small L<n> badge for the line where it was last assigned).
  • The current evaluated value, formatted the same way as the result gutter.

If a variable is reassigned later in the note, only the most recent value is shown — matching the way the evaluator updates the variable line by line as it works through your expressions.

Variables that errored or weren't fully defined yet are skipped, and the popup says "No variables defined yet." when the note doesn't have any.

Line references

Refer to the result of any line with L followed by the line number. Case doesn't matter — L1 and l1 both work:

1,000 + 250
L1 * 0.07

Line-ref popup: typing L on its own (not as part of a longer word) opens a picker showing every line that has a numeric result, e.g. L1: 1,250. Use the arrow keys to navigate, Enter or click to insert the reference. The popup follows the same flip-above / 5-item rules as the slash menu.

Single-click a line number to insert its ref. The numbers in the left gutter are buttons. Single-click one and its L<n> drops in at the cursor — double-click instead toggles that line between math and text. Inside an Excel-style function, clicking several line numbers in a row separates them with commas automatically (SUM( → click L1, L2, L3 → SUM(L1, L2, L3)); anywhere else it just inserts the bare ref so you supply the operators.

Empty lines count as 0. A reference to a blank line (or one with no numeric result) contributes 0 instead of erroring, just like an empty cell in Excel — so L5 + 10 is 10 when L5 is empty.

Forward references work too. A line on row 2 can reference L3 or any later line — the evaluator runs in passes until results stabilize, so forward refs resolve as long as there's no circular dependency.

Auto-shifting: when you insert or delete a whole line, every L<n> reference (including ranges like L1:L4) on surrounding lines is rewritten so they keep pointing at the same target.

Reference math from text

You can pull a math line's result into a plain text line by wrapping the line reference in double quotes — ""L1"". It renders as that line's live value and updates automatically whenever the source line changes:

running total so far: ""L13""

Put your cursor on the line to reveal the raw ""L1"" for editing; move off the line and it shows the result again. If the referenced line has no value yet, the raw token stays visible as a placeholder.

Line ranges (L1:L4)

Aggregate functions accept a colon-separated range of line refs that expands to "every line from A through B". Empty / blank / erroring lines inside the range are skipped, matching Excel's behavior:

10
20
30
SUM(L1:L3)        → 60
AVERAGE(L1:L3)    → 20

x as multiplication

The letter x works as a multiplication operator anywhere between numbers, parens, or other identifiers:

  • 5x10 → 50
  • (L1 + L2) x 3
  • $25 x 4 → 100

Because x is reserved as an operator, you can't define a variable called x. If you try, the result shows error with a hover tooltip explaining why.

Percent & basis points

% after a number means "of one": 50 * 20% → 10. With a leading + or - it becomes "relative to the left side":

  • 200 + 10% → 220 (10% of 200, added)
  • 200 - 5% → 190

bps (or bp) is basis points — one hundredth of a percent. Same relative behavior:

  • 1,000,000 + 25bps → 1,002,500
  • 50bps → 0.005

IF, TRUE, and FALSE

IF(condition, value_if_true, value_if_false) works the way you'd expect from Excel, with a few quality-of-life conveniences:

  • = means equality inside expressions — IF(L1=L2, …) compares L1 and L2. The line-level name = expr form is unaffected (that's still an assignment).
  • Bare TRUE and FALSE (uppercase) act as the string literals "TRUE" and "FALSE", so you don't have to wrap them in quotes: IF(L1=L2, TRUE, FALSE).
  • Defaults: if you omit the second and third arguments, IF returns "TRUE" when the condition is truthy and "FALSE" otherwise: IF(L1=L2) → TRUE or FALSE.
  • Other strings need quotes. If you write IF(L1=L2, YES, NO) the result shows err with a hover tooltip "Add Quotations" — wrap the values: IF(L1=L2, "YES", "NO").

Excel = prefix

If you have Excel muscle memory, you can prefix any formula with = and it's stripped before evaluation:

  • =SUM(L1:L4) — same as SUM(L1:L4)
  • =L1 * 1.05 — same as L1 * 1.05
  • =TODAY() — same as TODAY()

Find (Ctrl+F)

Press Ctrl+F to open a search bar in the top-right of the editor. Type to search the current note — every match is highlighted, with the current one in orange — and a counter shows 3/12 as you go.

  • Navigate — Enter / Shift+Enter, the ▲ / ▼ buttons, or F3 / Shift+F3. It wraps around and scrolls the current match into view.
  • Close — Esc or the ×; the cursor lands on the match you were viewing so you can keep editing there.
  • Handy touches — the bar prefills with any text you had selected, the box grows as you type, and the whole bar tints pale red when there are no matches. Searching is case-insensitive.
  • Ctrl+F while you're already typing in the box closes it; otherwise it (re)focuses the box and selects the text.

Error indicators

Problems are shown calmly in the inline result (right after the green =) only — the editor line itself is never highlighted or underlined in red, so typing stays quiet. Hover any marker for a tooltip explaining the issue.

There are just two visual styles:

  • N/A — a soft grey placeholder for a line that's simply incomplete, i.e. you're still mid-typing (a trailing operator, an unclosed SUM(, etc.). It's not really an error, so it stays quiet.
  • A muted, dotted-underlined marker for a genuine mistake. All of these share the same calm color (not an aggressive red):
    • error — the evaluator couldn't resolve the line: an unknown bare word (tooltip "Add Quotations" — wrap text values in quotes; only TRUE/FALSE work bare), assigning to x, or an otherwise invalid expression.
    • Excel Formula — you assigned to an Excel function name (sum = 5). The tooltip lists them.
    • Duplicate — that variable name is already defined on an earlier line; rename one of them.
    • Reserved — you assigned to a reserved name: a line ref (L1 = 5), a constant (pi = 5), or a literal (true = 5).

While a line is mid-edit and temporarily fails to parse, the previous good result is kept (shown slightly faded) instead of flashing an error.

Selection footer (Sum & Avg)

Highlight several lines in the editor and the status bar at the bottom shows the sum and average of the numeric results in your selection — handy for quick totals without writing a formula:

10

Lines without a numeric result are skipped. Single-line selections fall back to the normal "Ready" / "N errors" status.

Functions & constants

Powered by mathjs, so you have access to a large standard library. Common built-ins recognized by the syntax highlighter:

  • Constants: pi, e, tau, phi
  • Trig: sin, cos, tan, asin, acos, atan, atan2
  • Logs & exp: log, log2, log10, ln, exp
  • Roots & rounding: sqrt, abs, round, floor, ceil
  • Aggregates: min, max, sum, mean, median, mod

Examples: sqrt(2), sin(pi / 4), round(123.456, 1).

Markdown

Light markdown styling renders inline:

  • # Heading, ## Heading, ### Heading — header lines are not evaluated.
  • - item or * item — bulleted line. On a math line the content after the bullet is still evaluated. Enter continues the list, and Tab / Shift+Tab indent / outdent the item.
  • **bold** and *italic* — inline emphasis.

Tray & startup

  • Click the tray icon to toggle the popup. Double-click works too.
  • Right-click the tray icon for a menu: Open, Settings, Quit.
  • Closing the window only hides it — the app stays in the tray. Use the tray menu's "Quit" to exit.
  • "Launch when Windows starts" puts the app in your login items. It starts hidden, so only the tray icon appears until you click it.

Updating

Math Popup checks for updates automatically — shortly after launch and periodically while it's running — and downloads any new version in the background. When one is ready, a small blue dot appears on the ⚙ icon to let you know.

You can also check or install manually from Settings:

  1. Click the ⚙ icon in the header or right-click the tray icon to open Settings.
  2. Scroll to the bottom to find the About section.
  3. Click Check for Updates. The app will download the latest version in the background.
  4. Once downloaded, click Relaunch & Install to restart the app on the new version.

Settings

  • Theme — System (follows your OS), Light, or Dark. System mode updates automatically when you change your OS color scheme.
  • Launch when Windows starts — auto-start hidden in the tray.
  • Show taskbar icon — also show the window in the Windows taskbar so you can pin it and use it like a normal app. Off by default (it stays a tray-only app otherwise).
  • Auto-format numbers with commas — adds thousands separators as you type.
  • Expand suffixes in editor — turns 5m into 5,000,000 on space. Off keeps the typed form.
  • Max decimal places — caps how many decimals are shown in results. Integers omit the decimal point entirely.
  • Default zoom — the zoom level the popup opens at. Ctrl+0 returns to it; Ctrl+scroll or Ctrl++/− adjust it on the fly.
  • Custom suffixes — add your own multipliers (e.g. x = 1000, kg = 1, etc.). Each row has a symbol, multiplier, and case-sensitive toggle.

Storage & privacy

Everything is stored locally on your machine. Your note text, window position, and settings are saved to a settings.json file in the app's user-data folder. Nothing is sent over the network.

Your note auto-saves a few hundred milliseconds after you stop typing.
